Buyers look at the battery cell maker and capacity, stroke depth, noise, weight, grip material and pollutant testing: according to test.de, Stiftung Warentest found very high amounts of naphthalene in the plastic of one test device's control panel. For EU sales, battery transport testing (UN 38.3), the Batteries Regulation, ElektroG and GPSR matter; no therapeutic claims, otherwise medical device law applies.

Request from the supplier

  • Have the battery cell maker and capacity named (not stated in the listing) and request UN 38.3 and IEC 62133-2 reports; a lithium battery is dangerous goods (class 9) in sea and air freight.
  • PAH test report for housing, grip and control panel (Stiftung Warentest found naphthalene in the control panel of one device); the listing names no certificates, request CE/RoHS/FCC evidence.
  • CE documentation (LVD, EMC, RoHS) for the device and charging cable/adapter with EU plug (listing: 110-240 V).
  • Measure samples: noise in dB, stroke depth, weight, runtime; clarify the listing inconsistencies (6 vs. 8 heads; 4 constant levels + 3 auto modes).
  • Define the OEM configuration: levels, attachments, packaging, carry case, branding; if a larger battery is needed, cross-check the spec-heavier Todo TD00MG-3 (2,500 mAh, LCD).
  • Agree warranty (listing: one year) and return rate; no health-related promises (medical device law).
