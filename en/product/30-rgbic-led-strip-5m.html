Description

In an RGBIC strip the LED groups are individually controllable, so one roll runs several colours at once with smooth gradients and chasing effects.

More

The 5 m roll (5000 x 6 mm) can be cut every 15 LEDs per listing. Model KDY-PX2812-5V from Shenzhen Kediya (WS2812 chip family) is controlled by Bluetooth app and offers music sync and dimming; the range includes 5, 12 and 24 V versions as well as IP65 (silicone drip coating) and IP67 (silicone tube). Discerning buyers look for an EU-compliant power supply with test reports, even colours without voltage drop at the end of the roll, adhesive tape grip and an app without dropouts. Voice control needs a Wi-Fi controller (e.g. Tuya) to be specified; the listing only mentions Bluetooth.

Request from the supplier

  • Fix the variant: the listing mixes 5 V USB and 12/24 V versions; for a 5 m roll at full brightness choose 12 or 24 V (5 V needs feeding at both ends against voltage drop) and state the power-supply rating per metre in writing.
  • Request test reports (LVD/EN 62368-1 and EN 62031 for power supply and LED module, EMC EN IEC 55015, RED for a Wi-Fi controller, RoHS); the reports must name model and power supply, the listing only shows certificate names.
  • Clarify what the price includes: strip, controller, power supply, remote, connectors, packaging; the listing gives prices per metre, not per set.
  • Specify control: Bluetooth app (own or white-label), optionally Wi-Fi/Tuya for Alexa/Google, GDPR-compliant cloud, German language, firmware updates.
  • Measure series samples: colour uniformity over 5 m, brightness at the roll end, flicker when dimmed, tape adhesion at 20-40 °C, IP test for IP65 versions; record batch tolerances.
  • Clarify EPREL: whether the strip counts as a light source under Ecodesign (EU) 2019/2020 depends on construction and marketing; obtain an opinion from a test lab or the market surveillance authority and check the power supply under (EU) 2019/1782.
