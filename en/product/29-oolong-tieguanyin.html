Description

Tieguanyin is a semi-fermented oolong tea whose leaves are rolled into tight pellets that unfurl in hot water and give a pale yellow infusion with floral and fruity notes and a sweet aftertaste.

More

Discerning buyers expect uniformly round, heavy pellets with little breakage and dust, a clear infusion without foreign flavours and demonstrably low pesticide residues. As a food it needs no CE mark; the importer, as food business operator, is responsible for LMIV-compliant German labelling, traceability and residue evidence for every batch. Because tea from China is, according to the research, subject to increased pesticide checks under Regulation (EU) 2019/1793, a laboratory report belongs to each batch before shipment. The origin claim “Anxi” may go on the label only with proof.

Request from the supplier

  • Check original certificates (the supplier names an SGS audit and IMO-certified organic tea gardens as self-declaration): certifier, validity and scope tea.
  • Pesticide lab report per batch from an accredited laboratory (ISO/IEC 17025) under Regulation (EC) 396/2005, plus heavy metals and pyrrolizidine alkaloids under Regulation (EU) 2023/915, before shipment.
  • Proof of origin for tea garden and processing in Anxi (Fujian): the supplier is based in Hunan and mentions over 100 cooperating factories; without proof no “Anxi” claim.
  • Fix grade and sample in writing (listing: “Second grade”; cheaper grade “4th Grade EU Standard”, LHOT5003) and run an infusion test on appearance, colour and aroma.
  • Declaration of conformity for bags and inner bags under Regulation (EC) 1935/2004; check aroma and light protection, seal seam and batch code.
  • Approve the German label under LMIV in advance, register the importer as a food business operator, register packaging with LUCID (VerpackG); organic wording only with a control certificate under Regulation (EU) 2018/848.
