Description

Antistatic clothing to EN 1149-5 dissipates electrostatic charge through conductive fibres and prevents sparking in explosive atmospheres; it only works with an earthed wearer …

More

(dissipative footwear and floor) and when worn permanently closed. ESD coats to EN 61340-5-1, in contrast, protect electronic components from discharge. The listing describes a coat of polyester-cotton blend (TC 65/35) in cleanroom design (dust-proof class 100,000, ANSI/ESD S20.20) in several colours with customer logo; EN 1149-5 is not mentioned in the listing. Buyers in Germany expect test reports for surface resistance and charge decay (EN 1149-1/-3) and a construction without exposed metal parts.

More

VAT; listing ex works USD 8.20-9.60 per coat at MOQ 200 pieces. Target groups are electronics production, laboratories and printing shops (B2B); without a test report to EN 1149 the coat must not be advertised as 'EN 1149', return risk medium (fit, colour).

Request from the supplier

  • Request a test report to EN 1149-5 including EN 1149-3 (charge decay) or EN 1149-1; the listing only cites ANSI/ESD S20.20 - clarify to which standard testing was actually done.
  • Have the conductive fibre (carbon, stainless steel), grid, share and dissipative performance after the stated number of washes evidenced.
  • Check construction: no exposed metal parts (snaps, zip covered), marking and German instructions including a note on earthing the wearer.
  • If advertised as protective clothing for explosive areas, require EU type examination and declaration of conformity under 2016/425.
  • Clarify Jingmei's business type (manufacturer or trader), have the sample (USD 15) measured at a German laboratory.
