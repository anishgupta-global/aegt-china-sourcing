chrome metallic and confetti look plus butterfly stickers and is meant for garlands and arches at birthdays, weddings and baby showers. Chrome balloons drive the 'premium' look that German balloon shops explicitly promote for garlands. The supplier cites ISO 9001:2008, EN 71-1/-2/-3, CE and ASTM, a reject rate of about 2% and suitability for children aged 8 and over. For EU trade the warnings under EN 71-1 (choking risk from burst balloons), the migration test under EN 71-3 and registration under the German single-use plastics fund act are key, where balloons are covered.

Internal · notes

E-commerce potential

Ballonsupermarkt offers pastel, chrome, vintage and number balloons and explicitly promotes chrome balloons for garlands; Deiters lists balloons as add-ons to costumes.

More

Drugstores also carry DIY kits: dm sells the DECORAMI balloon garland 'pastel' (72 balloons, 5 m cord) for €19.95, and at PartyDeko.de metallic balloons in a 7-pack cost €1.49 (€0.21 each). The spread between purchase (about US$0.02 per balloon in the kit) and sale is wide, but competition on marketplaces is tough. Risks: bursts and colour deviation, warning-label obligations, seasonality (wedding and garden-party season).

Request from the supplier

  • Request test reports for EN 71-1, EN 71-3 and EN 71-12 for exactly this material (natural latex) and the chrome/confetti colours
  • Agree the EN 71-1 warning text in German on the packaging and the name/address of the EU responsible person (GPSR)
  • Fix the set bill of materials in writing: balloon sizes, chrome/confetti share, garland strip, glue dots, stickers
  • Clarify the material: latex or foil (page statement contradictory), test colour fastness and burst rate on a sample
  • Check whether balloons fall under EWKFondsG for the importer and clarify registration before first placing on the market
  • Evidence company status: the storefront names an office in Shijiazhuang, a factory in Ningjin (Xingtai) and cooperation with balloon makers, so ask for a factory audit
