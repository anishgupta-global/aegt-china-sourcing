Description

The handheld device massages the scalp with soft silicone nodes and vibration and works as a wellness add-on to daily hair care.

More

The housing (115 x 73 x 95 mm) sits compactly in the hand, and the 1,150 mAh battery charges in 2-3 hours. The maker calls the device waterproof; no specific IP rating is given in the listing. Discerning buyers look for skin-friendly silicone, a quiet motor, tightness in the shower and honest claims without hair-growth promises.

Per scan, MediaMarkt lists the Hiyori scalp massager (EUR 19.99 on 02.11.2026, marketplace) among hand massagers, and Notino carries hair-care devices next to dryers and stylers.

More

Web search confirms a fragmented marketplace landscape: offers from EUR 8.99 to EUR 85.95 on Kaufland.at, Comfier around EUR 76 (topratgeber24), Rybozen around EUR 30 (comparison site); IPX7 and red light are common selling points; test portals (testbedarf.de, topratgeber24) cover the category. No robust search volume was collected. A price point under EUR 20 = impulse buy in the hair-care basket with the dryer. Risks: battery life, tightness, impermissible hair-growth claims.

Request from the supplier

  • Demand an IP test report (e.g. IPX7 per EN 60529) for A456; without a report advertise only 'splash-proof' or similar.
  • Test reports EN 60335-1/-2-32, EMC; battery: UN 38.3 report, MSDS, charge protection.
  • Silicone and housing: suitability for skin contact (REACH, PAH, plasticisers), odour; request a material declaration.
  • Check company age: Made-in-China shows establishment 2025-11-05, 179 staff, 14,700 m2: demand business licence, SGS audit report and a video factory tour; ask about a predecessor entity.
  • Trial order at MOQ 50 (tier price US$7.92-8.59) before a series order; measure vibration strength, noise, run time.
  • No hair-growth or therapeutic claims in copy; German manual, GPSR details, ElektroG/battery/LUCID registrations.
