Description

The protected 18650 lithium-ion cell with built-in USB-C charging port (about 18.5 x 71 mm) is charged directly in the lamp or via cable from a power adapter …

More

which makes it the repeat-purchase item for flashlights and headlamps. Professional buyers expect protection against overcharge, deep discharge and short circuit, a measured capacity close to the stated value, a clean button-top contact for all common lamps and a charge indicator; the reference article Nitecore NL1836R offers 3,600 mAh at 3.6 V, 6 A continuous discharge and a USB-C input of 5 V/1 A (manufacturer data). Legally a battery is its own, strictly regulated product: conformity and labelling under the Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 (obligations apply in stages, some only from 2027), test reports to EN IEC 62133-2 and UN 38.3, registration in Germany and shipment as dangerous goods class 9. From China the cell should only be bought with a test report for exactly this model and with an EU responsible person.

E-commerce potential

Demand follows the lamp: flashlights and headlamps rise in autumn (Tchibo offers a rechargeable headlamp for EUR 6 timed to the clock change on 25 Oct 2026, netzwelt of 20 Oct 2026) …

More

and every lamp with an 18650 cell eventually needs a replacement. Market prices: Nitecore NL1836R EUR 34.95 gross at Voelkner (checked), EUR 23.27 plus EUR 3.95 shipping at military.eu per search result (page blocked on fetch); Voelkner sells the spare battery as a separate item next to the lamps. The China list price of the Vapcell counterpart is USD 4.85 to 5.20 per cell, so the margin room is large, but a brand premium like Nitecore's cannot be enforced one to one. Risks: dangerous-goods shipping (class 9), product liability in case of fire, battery registration and returns because button top and length do not fit every lamp.

Request from the supplier

  • Measure capacity on samples yourself (discharge test, target at least 95 % of the stated value) and request a dimension drawing with length and diameter including button top.
  • Test reports for exactly this cell/PCB model: EN IEC 62133-2 and UN 38.3 (summary) from an accredited lab, plus safety data sheet (MSDS).
  • Have the protection circuit (overcharge, deep discharge, short circuit, overcurrent) and thermal fuse confirmed in writing; ask for the cell origin (branded or no-name) and batch traceability.
  • Clarify labelling under the Batteries Regulation on cell and packaging: type, capacity, manufacturer with address, batch number, disposal symbol; German notices and EU responsible person.
  • Check the charging electronics: charge cut-off at 4.2 V, charge indicator, USB-C socket mechanically robust; measure heat generation while charging.
  • Define dangerous-goods packaging and mode of transport (cell shipped at no more than 30 % state of charge, UN 38.3 compliant packaging, air or sea freight).
