Asian groceries & snacks

Plant-based Asian pantry staples that German and EU shoppers keep buying online: seasoning oils and sauces, noodles, tea, bubble-tea ingredients and snacks.

More

The range runs from evergreens (soy sauce, glass noodles, shiitake) to trend items (chili crisp, boba pearls, konjac noodles). Meant for AE Global Traders and independent online sellers who want to add private-label or direct-import lines from China to branded goods; deliberately without meat, dairy, egg and fish, because importing these from China into the EU is effectively blocked or requires approved establishments.

Only Guizhou (Guiyang) as the birthplace of the chili-crisp genre is source-backed here (Wikipedia: Lao Gan Ma, since 1997). All other assignments are industry knowledge and must be confirmed during supplier vetting: soy sauce and seasoning sauces Guangdong (Foshan, Zhongshan, Jiangmen); chili oils also Sichuan/Chongqing; glass noodles Longkou (Shandong), sweet-potato noodles Sichuan/Chongqing; oolong tea Anxi and Wuyishan (Fujian), Chaozhou (Guangdong); shiitake Henan, Zhejiang (Qingyuan), Hubei, Fujian; konjac Sichuan, Yunnan, Hubei; bubble-tea ingredients and mochi Guangdong (Guangzhou, Dongguan, Shenzhen) and Fujian; sesame oil Henan, Shandong, Anhui; Sichuan pepper Hanyuan and Jinyang (Sichuan), Wudu (Gansu).

More

Also check flavourings and fillings (beef/chicken flavour, egg-yolk filling, fish sauce, oyster sauce, gelatine); a vegan claim needs recipe evidence. 2) Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/1793: per the consolidated text (umwelt-online, page dated 12 June 2026, latest amendment seen Reg. (EU) 2026/459) China is subject to increased controls for, among others, tea (CN 0902, pesticide residues, 20 %), peanuts and peanut products (aflatoxins) and ground sweet pepper (ex 0904 22 00, Salmonella); Annex II lists xanthan (ethylene oxide). The lists are reviewed at least every six months: re-check on EUR-Lex before every order; whether chili powder in blends falls under the pepper entry must be clarified case by case. 3) Labelling under (EU) 1169/2011 in German (allergens soy, wheat, sesame, peanut, sulphite highlighted), best-before date, name and address of the EU food business operator; contaminants (EU) 2023/915 (incl. 3-MCPD in soy sauce, mycotoxins, heavy metals in mushrooms); pesticides (EC) 396/2005 (ethylene oxide residues are a known issue in sesame and spices); food contact (EC) 1935/2004 / LFGB; packaging registration LUCID (VerpackG); importer registered as food business operator, traceability and HACCP. 4) Product-specific: konjac jelly in mini-cups (E 425) is not marketable in the EU, konjac noodles are allowed; plastic straws in bubble-tea kits are banned under Directive (EU) 2019/904; add a choking-hazard note for mochi and konjac. 5) Logistics: sea freight takes weeks; fix remaining shelf life on arrival and batch analyses contractually.
