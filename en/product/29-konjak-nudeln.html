Description

Konjac noodles (shirataki) consist in essence of konjac flour, water and an acidity regulator and are sold as pre-cooked wet goods in a bag with brine; they are very low in calories …

More

(listing: 6 kcal per serving), neutral in taste and are only rinsed and briefly heated before eating. Discerning buyers expect a neutral smell after rinsing, even, non-sticky strands, tight seal seams and a clearly stated drained weight. As a food it needs no CE mark; the importer is responsible for LMIV labelling, correct additive declaration (konjac, acidity regulator E 526) and residue evidence. What is evidenced is the range depth of the low-carb niche in German Asian retail, not its sales volume.

More

the Sun Plus konjac noodles cost 2.89 EUR per 200 g there (14.45 EUR per kg). The China goods are at 0.42-0.55 USD per 200 g bag, but water weight (about 8.1 kg gross per 30-bag carton), 12.8 % duty, private-label packaging and lab costs narrow the margin. Competition: established brands in Asian retail; differentiate through variety (plain, spinach, seaweed, oat per listing) and neutral smell. Risks: brine smell and texture create review and return risk; shelf life over 12 months per listing, check heat exposure in sea freight.

Request from the supplier

  • Fix recipe and ingredient list in writing (konjac flour share, calcium hydroxide E 526, no further additives, flavour varieties); claims such as gluten-free, sugar-free or fat-free only with lab proof.
  • Clarify origin and treatment of the konjac flour (the supplier names its own konjac farm at the Jinsha River), including sulphur treatment (sulphite labelling); lab report per batch on pesticides (Regulation (EC) 396/2005) and contaminants (Regulation (EU) 2023/915).
  • Check the wet pack: heat treatment or sterilisation, seal seam, tightness and shelf life (over 12 months per listing) in a transport trial with sea freight and heat load; state drained weight and brine.
  • Sensory samples in at least two varieties: smell after rinsing, bite, sticking; cooking test following the supplier’s instructions.
  • Original certificates (BRC, IFS, HACCP, Halal, Kosher) and factory audit (BV audit per profile); EU organic only with a control certificate under Regulation (EU) 2018/848.
  • Approve the German label under LMIV with preparation and consumption note (the supplier recommends 125 g per meal with one to two glasses of water) in advance; register packaging with LUCID (VerpackG); secure the customs classification via binding tariff information (vZTA).
