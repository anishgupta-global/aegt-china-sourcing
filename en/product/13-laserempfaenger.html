Description

The laser receiver makes the beam of a rotary laser measurable where the eye can no longer see it: in sunlight, outdoors and at longer range.

More

A digital display of the laser-line position with a zero function shows whether the receiver is above, below or on laser height; the signal comes via display and LED and, on many branded models, also audibly. On site, a robust housing, a holder for levelling staff or tripod and compatibility with red and green laser light matter. Professional buyers expect an EMC- and RoHS-compliant unit, clean battery removal, German documentation and a receiver whose pulse detection matches the rotary laser in use.

E-commerce potential

Demand follows the rotary laser: Hornbach carries nine models in its laser receiver category from EUR 99.95 (Bosch LR 7) to EUR 390.98 (Bosch LR 65 G), and the Stabila REC 160 RG is EUR 169.00.

More

Chinese receivers are listed on Accio from USD 18 (Huipu HRD100G, green only, USD 18 to 25); red/green models range from USD 19 to 60, well below the brand price. Competition comes from brands with system lock-in (Bosch, Stabila, Laserliner); the biggest return risk is a mismatched pulse detection, so sell only with a compatibility list and a clear statement of laser colour. Bestseller ranks were not checked.

Request from the supplier

  • Compatibility test with the target rotary lasers (e.g. LAISAI LS535), red and green, pulse detection confirmed in writing
  • Data sheet with range, receiving window length, fine/coarse accuracy and ingress protection (IP code); not published for the LS7017
  • EU declaration of conformity with test reports on EMC (EN 61326-1) and RoHS from an accredited lab
  • Battery type, removability and marking under (EU) 2023/1542; registration under BattG and ElektroG before selling
  • Holder (clamp or magnet) for levelling staff and tripod in the delivery, German manual, GPSR details with an EU responsible person
  • No copy of design or logo of Bosch, Stabila or Laserliner; sample before series
